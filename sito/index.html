What it actually does

No thirty-field forms. Each screen does one thing, and does it with a phone in one hand and a bottle in the other.

📋

Count by station

Main bar, walk-in, basement — each person counts their own area. Slider or keypad, partial bottles included. At the end you only look at what's off.

📊

Expected vs. actual

Barback knows what you should have from deliveries and empties logged. You count what's really there. That gap is the most useful number in the app.

🛒

Orders that write themselves

When a product drops below par it lands on the order list, grouped by vendor. The message is already written — send it as is.

📷

Barcode scanning

Point the phone at the bottle and it comes right up. Scan an unknown barcode once, link it to a product, and it knows it from then on.

📆

Scheduling and time off

Shifts in a grid, side work you can check off. Staff request time off in the app, and whoever approves sees immediately which shifts would go uncovered.

📱

Phone and desktop

Installs from the browser onto a phone or a computer and lives with your apps. Everyone gets their own PIN and sees only what their role allows.

What it doesn't do

I'd rather tell you now than have you find out in two months.

  • It doesn't connect to your POS. Pours get captured when you log empties, or they show up as variance at the next count. It works, but it's an extra step — and if POS-synced depletion is what you need, buy something else.
  • It's not on the App Store or Google Play. It installs from the browser and sits with your apps, icon and all — but that's not the same thing and I won't pretend it is.
  • It doesn't run multiple locations under one login. Today it's one install per location, each with its own address and its own separate database.
  • It doesn't work offline. The screen still opens; your data doesn't.
  • It's not accounting, invoicing, or plate costing. It does beverage inventory and staff. Your bookkeeper keeps the rest.

Questions I get asked

If yours isn't here, text me — you'll get me, not a ticket queue.

How long until we're up and running?

Setup runs about half an hour: the products you carry, your vendors, and the stations where you count. On Done For You I do that before I hand it over, so you walk into something already configured.

Your first full count takes a solid hour, because you have to count everything once. After that it's twenty minutes.

My bar manager is sixty and hates apps.

To count, there's nothing to learn: open your station, drag a slider, move to the next bottle. No keyboard, no menus to hunt through.

And they only see what they need. A bartender profile doesn't even show the scheduling or vendor screens.

Where does my data live?

On a US server, in a database that's yours, separate from every other bar's. There's no shared pool — even if I wanted to, I couldn't show your numbers to anyone else.

Your data stays yours, and you can download a complete copy any time from a button inside the app. If you ever leave, you take it with you.

What if the internet goes down mid-service?

You count once a week, not during a rush. If the line drops, count later — the bottles aren't going anywhere.

But you should know up front: with no connection the app opens and that's it.

Why pay when Backbar has a free tier?

Because the free tier counts bottles and stops there, and it doesn't do staff scheduling at all.

Their plan that does what Barback does runs $129 a month. This is $39, it includes scheduling and time off, and it works in Spanish for your back of house.

Where they win: POS integration. If that's your deal-breaker, they're the better buy and I'll tell you so.

What if you shut down in six months?

Fair question, and you get the real answer instead of a reassurance. Your data sits in a file you can download whenever you want — you're never held hostage.

It's in the agreement: if I wind down, you get a complete export and thirty days' notice. That's the least I owe you.
